# Scenario Methodology

## Purpose

Scenario planning tests management decisions under coherent operating conditions. It is not a collection of isolated percentage changes.

## Scenario hierarchy

| Scenario | Interpretation |
|---|---|
| Upside | Stronger volume and price/mix, healthier margin, faster cash conversion |
| Base | Current rolling-forecast assumptions |
| Downside | Lower demand, margin pressure, slower collections and inventory movement |
| Stress | Severe commercial and working-capital deterioration with defensive actions |

Expected ordering is enforced:

```text
Upside > Base > Downside > Stress
```

for both revenue and EBITDA.

## Connected drivers

- Revenue volume
- Price and channel mix
- Gross margin / COGS
- Payroll and hiring
- Marketing and other opex
- DSO, DIO, and DPO
- Capex timing
- Cash from operations and ending cash

## Decision triggers

Management should define thresholds for revenue gap, gross margin, EBITDA, minimum cash, and CCC. When a threshold is breached, the action plan may include commercial recovery, pricing review, hiring controls, capex deferral, collections acceleration, inventory reduction, or supplier-term negotiation.
